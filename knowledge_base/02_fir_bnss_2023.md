# FIR and inquest after a factory death

> Replaces the blog-post-derived FIR file in the original corpus. That file
> cited s.154(3), s.156(3), s.169, s.190 CrPC and s.182 IPC — **all
> repealed on 1 July 2024**. Delete it; do not patch it.

---

## chunk: bnss_194_inquest
`verification: verified_secondary` — **the most important provision in this file**

*Families ask: they took his body away, what should we do? The police have
taken the body. They say we can take him home now. Do we need a post-mortem?
The factory is saying there is no need for all this. Can we do the cremation
today? What happens to the body after a factory accident?*

**Do not let the body go without a post-mortem.** This is the most
time-critical thing in this entire knowledge base — the evidence disappears
within hours and cannot be recovered afterwards.

In figures: the inquest report is due within **24 hours (twenty-four
hours)**.

**Section 194 BNSS — Police to enquire and report on suicide, etc.**

This is the provision that actually governs a factory death, and it was
entirely absent from the original corpus.

**Sub-section (1).** Where a police officer receives information that a
person has died by suicide, **or has been killed by an animal, by machinery,
or by an accident**, or has died in circumstances raising reasonable
suspicion that some other person has committed an offence, the officer shall
**immediately give intimation to the nearest Executive Magistrate**, and
shall proceed to the place, investigate in the presence of witnesses, and
record the apparent cause of death and any marks of injury.

**Sub-section (2).** The report shall be signed by the police officer and
the other persons present, and forwarded to the District Magistrate or
Sub-divisional Magistrate **within twenty-four hours**.

**Sub-section (3).** The body shall be sent to the nearest Civil Surgeon or
other qualified medical person for **post-mortem examination** where there
is doubt as to the cause of death, or where the police officer considers it
expedient. (Sub-section (3) also sets out mandatory categories concerning
the death of a woman within seven years — 7 years — of marriage.)

**Sub-section (4).** District Magistrates, Sub-divisional Magistrates and
specially empowered Executive Magistrates may hold inquests.

> **Why this matters for your users.** "Killed by machinery or by an
> accident" is a textual fit for a factory death. This means:
>
> - The police were required to inform an Executive Magistrate immediately.
> - An inquest report was required within 24 hours.
> - A post-mortem should follow where the cause of death is in doubt.
>
> A family that is told "there's nothing to investigate, just take the body"
> is being told something the law does not support. **The post-mortem report
> and the inquest report are the two documents that later determine whether
> compensation and criminal liability are established.** If the family lets
> the body go without a post-mortem, that evidence is gone permanently.
>
> This is arguably the single most time-critical thing the bot can tell
> anyone, because it expires within hours.

## chunk: bnss_173_fir_registration
`verification: verified_primary`

**Section 173 BNSS — Information in cognizable cases.**

**(1)** Every item of information relating to the commission of a cognizable
offence may be given **orally or by electronic communication**, irrespective
of the area where the offence is committed (this is the "Zero FIR"
principle). If given orally, it shall be reduced to writing, read over to
the informant, and signed by them.

**(2)** "A copy of the information as recorded under sub-section (1) shall
be given **forthwith, free of cost**, to the informant or the victim."

**(3)** For offences punishable with three (3) years or more but less than
seven (7) years, the officer may — with the prior permission of an officer
not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police — conduct a
**preliminary enquiry within fourteen days (14 days)** to ascertain whether a
prima facie case exists.

> Note for the bot: sub-section (3) is a common source of delay. A family
> told "we are doing a preliminary enquiry" is not necessarily being
> stonewalled — but the fourteen-day limit is real and is a legitimate thing
> to ask about once it passes.

## chunk: bnss_173_4_sp_escalation
`verification: verified_primary` — **replaces the wrong s.154(3) CrPC citation**

*Families ask: the police are refusing to write the FIR. They sent us away.
The thana is not registering the case. They keep telling us to come back
tomorrow. The police are taking the factory's side. What do we do if the
police won't listen?*

**You can go over their heads, in writing, and they are then obliged to act.**

**Section 173(4) BNSS — when the police refuse to register.**

Where a police officer refuses to record the information, the aggrieved
person may **send the substance of that information, in writing and by post,
to the Superintendent of Police concerned**. If the Superintendent is
satisfied that the information discloses the commission of a cognizable
offence, they shall either investigate the case themselves or direct an
investigation by a subordinate officer.

If still no action follows, the person may approach a Magistrate.

> **This is your MVP's escalation step.** The original corpus cited
> "s.154(3) CrPC" for it — a repealed provision. The correct citation is
> **s.173(4) BNSS**.
>
> Practical points the bot should carry: it must be **in writing**, it must
> go **by post** (keep the receipt), and the family should keep a copy.
> Ludhiana is a Police Commissionerate, so the equivalent officer may be a
> **Deputy Commissioner of Police / Commissioner of Police** rather than an
> SP — the bot should name both and let the family ask locally.

**Section 175(3) BNSS — Magistrate's power to order investigation.**

The successor to the old s.156(3) CrPC route: a Magistrate empowered to take
cognizance may order an investigation.

> ⚠️ Verbatim text not retrieved. It is understood that BNSS adds
> preconditions absent from the old s.156(3) — including that the applicant
> must first have exhausted the s.173(4) route and must support the
> application by an affidavit. **Confirm the exact preconditions before the
> bot tells anyone to go to a Magistrate**, because sending a family to
> court prematurely will get their application dismissed and cost them
> weeks.

## chunk: bns_105_106_offence_sections
`verification: verified_secondary`

**Which sections a factory-death FIR is registered under.**

In figures: s.106(1) carries up to **7 years**; s.106(2) up to **10 years**;
s.105 is **5 to 10 years** or life.

**BNS s.105 — Punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder.**
"Whoever commits culpable homicide not amounting to murder, shall be
punished with imprisonment for life, or imprisonment of either description
for a term which shall not be less than five years but which may extend to
ten years, and shall also be liable to fine" — where the act is done with
intent to cause death. Where done with knowledge that it is likely to cause
death but without intent: up to ten years and fine.

**BNS s.106(1) — Causing death by negligence.** "Whoever causes the death of
any person by doing any rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable
homicide, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a
term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to fine."

**BNS s.106(2)** — where the person causing death **escapes from the scene
or fails to report the incident** to a police officer or Magistrate soon
after: up to ten years and fine.

> **Most factory-death FIRs are registered under s.106(1).** Families
> frequently ask "what section is the case under?" and the answer tells them
> a lot about how seriously the police are treating it.
>
> A judgement the bot should NOT make: whether the facts amount to s.105
> rather than s.106. That is a lawyer's call and a serious escalation. The
> bot should explain the difference neutrally and hand off.

## chunk: bns_217_false_information
`verification: verified_secondary`

**BNS s.217** replaces the old s.182 IPC (giving false information to a
public servant). The original corpus cited "s.182 IPC" — repealed.

> Handle with care. This is the section a family might be *threatened with*
> by someone trying to discourage them from filing. The bot should not lead
> with it. If raised, the framing is: giving information in good faith is
> not an offence, even if it later proves mistaken.

## chunk: what_the_family_should_secure
`verification: derived` — practical guidance, needs legal partner sign-off

**Documents to secure early.** (Not legal advice; a checklist.)

1. **Free copy of the FIR** — s.173(2) BNSS entitles them to it, free, at
   once.
2. **Post-mortem report** — flows from s.194(3) BNSS.
3. **Inquest report** — s.194(2) BNSS, filed within 24 hours.
4. **The employer's fatal-accident report** — s.73 Code on Social Security,
   due within seven days (7 days).
5. **The employer's accident notice to the Inspector-cum-Facilitator**
   (FORM-XI where the Central Rules apply) — see `04_employer_duties.md`.
6. **Proof of employment** — ID card, wage slips, attendance record, bank
   credits, contractor's name, photographs at the workplace, co-worker
   contact details.

> Point 6 is where these cases are actually won or lost. Ludhiana units
> frequently deny the deceased ever worked there, especially where the
> worker was engaged through a contractor or paid in cash. **The bot should
> raise proof-of-employment early and urgently — before anything else gets
> cleaned up.**
